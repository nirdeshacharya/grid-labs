Grid Labs

A notebook of experiments on SSEN's open network data

Each entry asks one question of the data published by Scottish and Southern Electricity Networks, shows how it was answered, and says what the answer cannot support.

Grid Labs combines that data with other public sources to test ideas, so results here are provisional and some will turn out to be wrong.

  1. 01

    Is more generation contracted than the grid supply points can carry?

    Connected and contracted generation at each grid supply point, set against the transformer capacity installed there.

    FindingPosted 24 October 2026 · recomputed from SSEN's data each time the page opens
  2. 02

    At what wind speed do faults take off, and which places are most sensitive?

    Ten years of high-voltage fault records matched day by day to the strongest gust in each of SSEN's 31 districts.

    FindingPosted 29 October 2026 · snapshot
  3. 04

    Where could home electrification use up substation headroom before upgrades arrive?

    Spare demand capacity at each primary set against SSEN's own projections of heat pumps, electric vehicles and new homes, with adjustable assumptions.

    FindingPosted 3 November 2026 · snapshot, recalculated as you change the settings
  4. 05

    Once you allow for how much overhead line each place has, where does the network fault most?

    Ten years of overhead line faults divided by the length of line they happened on, by district and by feeder, with what causes them and whether bad feeders stay bad.

    FindingPosted 15 November 2026 · snapshot
  5. 06

    How much Orkney wind does active network management switch off?

    Ten years of half-hourly output from the generators Orkney's network can turn down, set against the wind and the generators it never turns down. Three estimates, and a gap the public data cannot explain.

    FindingPosted 18 November 2026 · snapshot
  6. 07

    Are heat pumps and home chargers arriving as fast as SSEN's 2020 scenarios assumed?

    SSEN's 2020 projections for the end of 2023, against its later baseline estimate and its own connection records, for both licence areas and every southern council.

    FindingPosted 21 November 2026 · snapshot
  7. 08

    Do trees near lines explain the south's higher fault rate?

    Satellite tree cover read every 100 m along 32,000 km of 11 kV line, against ten years of tree faults. More trees explain part of the gap; calm-day faults explain more.

    FindingPosted 21 November 2026 · snapshot
