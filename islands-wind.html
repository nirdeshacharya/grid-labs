Are the islands tougher, or just windier?

Each line is one of SSEN's 31 fault-reporting districts: how many faults it has on a day, compared with its calmer days, as the wind rises. Switch how the wind is measured and watch where the islands go.

Measure the wind by

Hover or tap a line to see which district it is.

How to read it

The height of a line is a district's faults per day on days with that wind, divided by its faults per day on its calmer half of days. So 1× is a normal day for that place and 10× is ten times as many faults. The scale is logarithmic, so each step up is a multiplication.

In mph, every district shares the same scale. The other view ranks each district's days against its own ten years of weather: the far right is roughly the windiest day of an average year there, whatever speed that is. On Orkney that is about 80 mph; in Reading it is under 60.

What it shows

This is the finding from entry 02, which has the method, robustness checks and limits in full. The one that matters most here: each district gets one weather point, so the wind it records is a smoothed estimate, and large northern districts will have seen stronger gusts than the lines suggest.
