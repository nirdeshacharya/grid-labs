Do trees near lines explain the south's higher fault rate?

A satellite map of tree cover, read every 100 m along 32,000 km of 11 kV overhead line, set against ten years of faults SSEN put down to trees.

Entry 05 found southern England's 11 kV lines fault about two-fifths more often per kilometre than the north of Scotland's, and that trees cause most of the difference. The obvious explanation is that the south's lines run past more trees. This entry measures that.
